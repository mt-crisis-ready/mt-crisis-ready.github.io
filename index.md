Communication in times of crisis is essential. However, there is often a mismatch between the language of governments, aid providers, doctors, and those to whom they are providing aid. Commercial MT systems are reasonable tools to turn to in these scenarios. But how effective are these tools for translating to and from low resource languages, particularly in the crisis or medical domain? 

In the study, **Is Machine Translation Ready for the Next Crisis or Pandemic** [(Bansal et al., 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10082), a paper to be presented at the first annual [Language Technology for Crisis Preparedness or Response (LT4CPR)](https://lt4cpr.github.io/aacl2026-workshop-LT4CPR/) workshop (co-located with [AACL-IJCNLP](https://2026.aaclnet.org/) in Hengqin, China in November 2026), we evaluate the quality five existing Machine Translation services, namely, Google Translate, Microsoft Translator, GPT, Gemini and Claude across 36 different language pairs (with English) to determine if these services are "usable" in the context of a crisis, in particular, in a pandemic.


This website contains a release of the material from [(Bansal et al., 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10082), an archival paper appearing in the [LT4CPR workshop](https://lt4cpr.github.io/aacl2026-workshop-LT4CPR/index.html) of [AACL-IJCNLP 2026](https://2026.aaclnet.org/).
