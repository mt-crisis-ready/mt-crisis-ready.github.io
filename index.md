Communication in times of crisis is essential. However, there is often a mismatch between the language of governments, aid providers, doctors, and those to whom they are providing aid. Commercial MT systems are reasonable tools to turn to in these scenarios. But how effective are these tools for translating to and from low resource languages, particularly in the crisis or medical domain? 

In the study, **Is Machine Translation Ready for the Next Crisis or Pandemic** [(Bansal et al., 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10082), a paper to be presented at the first annual [Language Technology for Crisis Preparedness or Response (LT4CPR)](https://lt4cpr.github.io/aacl2026-workshop-LT4CPR/) workshop (co-located with [AACL-IJCNLP](https://2026.aaclnet.org/) in Hengqin, China in November 2026), the authors evaluated the quality of five existing Machine Translation services. These services, namely, Google Translate, Microsoft Translator, GPT, Gemini and Claude, were evaluated against the [TICO-19 benchmark test set]( https://tico-19.github.io/). The TICO-19 benchmark contains pandemic and medically related content that are n-ways parallel across 36 different languages, many of which are low- to medium-resource languages spoken in Africa and Southeast Asia. The goal of [Bansal et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10082) was to determine the "usability" of these commercial MT tools in crisis and pandemic settings across the entire set of TICO-19 languages when paired with English (both from and to). This was done by evaluating the quality of the output from these engines across multiple metrics, and then setting a threshold of usability (see the paper for more details).

This repository was created to host **all** data that was translated as part of the [Bansal et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10082) study. Also included are all code used to retrieve the data from the various services is also included, as well as code used to evaluate the output. Evaluation metrics that were used include BLEU, CHRF, CHRF++, BertScore and COMET. (A complete set of scores across all languages can be found in the appendices in the [Bansal et al., 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10082 paper.)
